Where the schedule broke down

At UC Riverside, staff had to keep games moving through busy evening blocks while managing facility capacity and participant flow. Repeated late starts signaled a scheduling problem. We needed to see when delays clustered and where coverage or league formats could be adjusted.

My contribution

As operations manager, I supervised 15–20 student employees and coordinated 8–12 weekly leagues and open recreation blocks. I built the Excel and R reports, identified recurring delays, and used the findings to support the team’s staffing and scheduling adjustments.

From nightly logs to weekly decisions

I tracked participation, forfeits, incidents, staffing coverage, and schedule performance. In the weekly reviews, I surfaced repeated late starts, peak-window bottlenecks, and coverage gaps. The reports also indicated which league formats saw recurring bottlenecks.

What the operation changed

The team used those patterns to guide staffing and schedule adjustments. Over two quarters, the recreation operation recorded a 20% reduction in late game starts while maintaining 95%+ on-time completion.

What I took from the work

The timing metrics reflect the team’s performance across the changes. Isolating the effect of one adjustment would require evaluating that change on its own. Tracking starts and completions together gave the team a better way to judge whether the full evening schedule was getting more reliable.
