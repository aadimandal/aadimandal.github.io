Business problem

Reviewing accounts in an arbitrary order can bury borrowers at higher risk of default. A lender also needs to understand how changes to approval and monitoring rules could affect volume, potential losses, and risk-adjusted profitability.

My role

I built the SQL portfolio analysis and Python model pipeline, compared credit-policy scenarios, and created a Tableau dashboard to communicate risk concentration and policy tradeoffs.

Method

I used the UCI Default of Credit Card Clients dataset. I profiled exposure, expected loss, and profitability proxies in DuckDB, then compared logistic regression, random forest, and histogram gradient boosting. The strongest model achieved a holdout ROC-AUC of 0.779 and 3.15× default lift in the highest-risk holdout decile.

I simulated conservative, balanced, and growth-oriented policies to compare approval volume, default capture, and risk-adjusted profit proxies under different screening rules.

Decision informed

The risk ranking showed how to direct review toward accounts with greater predicted default risk. In the full-portfolio validation, a 0.150 monitoring threshold flagged 49.94% of accounts and captured 86.38% of historical defaults.

That threshold illustrates a review-capacity tradeoff using historical data. Loss and profit are proxy estimates because the dataset lacks actual pricing, recoveries, and charge-offs. The policy scenarios support portfolio comparison.
