The business question

A risk team needs to know how many accounts that later defaulted would have surfaced at each level of review capacity. Policy teams face a related question. Tightening a rule changes the number of accounts approved and the risks the portfolio retains. I used the same portfolio to examine both decisions.

My contribution

I built the SQL analysis in DuckDB and the Python modeling pipeline. I profiled exposure, expected-loss proxies, and profitability proxies, then compared logistic regression, random forest, and histogram gradient boosting on a stratified holdout. I used Tableau to show the review and approval tradeoffs. UCI dataset.

What the holdout showed

Histogram gradient boosting led the comparison. Its highest-risk holdout decile defaulted at 69.67%, against 22.12% across the holdout, for 3.15× lift. This showed how a ranked queue could concentrate a limited review effort on accounts with more observed defaults. Holdout evaluation.

How to size the review queue

In the full-portfolio analysis, a monitoring threshold of 0.150 flagged 49.94% of accounts and captured 86.38% of historical defaults. I compared conservative, balanced, and growth-oriented scenarios to show what changes as a team accepts more accounts or allocates more reviews. The threshold provides a reference point for capacity planning.

What a lender would still need to test

The full-portfolio view includes training records, and I used the holdout to select the model. A lender would need to validate performance on a later, independent sample, check results across customer groups, and size the cutoff to its review capacity. The financial scenarios use proxies because the data lacks actual pricing, recoveries, and charge-offs.
