Business problem

Retention budgets are finite. Some high-risk customers have too little value at stake to justify a discount, and some people who accept an offer would have stayed anyway. The business needed a way to prioritize spend and measure incremental impact.

My role

I built the customer-level dataset, estimated profit-adjusted customer lifetime value (CLV), trained and validated the churn model, and turned the scores into an offer screen, a budget simulation, and an A/B test plan.

Method

Using Python and SQL, I combined predicted churn, a profit-adjusted CLV proxy, and lifecycle context. I checked how the model’s ranking held up after removing direct cancellation signals. For paid offers, I multiplied churn probability by the CLV proxy and the assumed intervention response rate, then subtracted estimated offer cost.

I varied response rates, costs, and budget limits. Under base-case assumptions, 71,437 of 970,960 scored customers cleared the paid-offer screen. The $750K cap captured 99.99% of the maximum net value in the tested budget range. That figure is a simulation result based on value and cost proxies.

Decision informed

I proposed a stratified A/B test using 62,836 eligible customers, planned around the $750K cap. Treatment would receive the offer and control would establish how much retention would have happened anyway.

The rollout criteria require lower churn, positive incremental net value, and acceptable customer-experience measures. The project ended with the proposed test design and modeled economics.
